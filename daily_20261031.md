### 1. 一个具有细粒度可解释性的临床多模态视觉语言基础模型。

- **英文标题**: A clinical multimodal vision language foundation model with fine-grain explainability.
- **期刊**: Nature medicine (2026)
- **作者**: Yang Bai, Haoran Cheng, Yang Zhou
- **类别**: 医学影像 AI
- **相关性**: 6/10 — 评分失败，默认保留
- **原文链接**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42957777/

**中文摘要**: 尽管当前的许多医学基础模型前景可观，但仍具有模态特异性且缺乏透明推理，限制了其临床采用。为弥补这一空白，我们提出可解释医学视觉语言基础模型（Explainable Vision Language Foundation Model for Medicine, EVLF-FM），这是一种将广泛诊断能力与细粒度可解释性相结合的多模态视觉语言基础模型。EVLF-FM的开发与测试涵盖了来自18个全球数据集的超过130万份总样本，涉及10种成像模态，并与六个临床专科相关：皮肤科、肝病科、眼科、病理科、肺科和放射科。外部验证使用了来自17个额外数据集的13,443份独立测试样本，涵盖10种成像模态。EVLF-FM支持疾病诊断、视觉问答以及具有推理能力的像素级视觉定位。在疾病诊断的内部验证中，EVLF-FM取得了最高的平均准确率0.895，优于领先的通用型和专科型模型。在医学视觉定位中，EVLF-FM也在九种模态上取得了合理性能，在交并比阈值为0.5时的平均准确率（Acc@0.5）为0.842。外部测试进一步证实了其稳定的零样本和少样本性能，尽管模型规模较小，仍具有有竞争力的F1分数。一种结合监督微调和视觉强化微调的混合训练策略，使EVLF-FM能够生成与视觉证据对齐的临床可解释阐释。使用S.C.O.R.E.框架对这些推理输出进行人工评估，总体平均分高达4.25/5，支持其临床可解释性。当前局限性包括需要在多样化医疗环境中进行额外验证。在常规临床部署之前，需要开展前瞻性临床研究以评估EVLF-FM的安全性、有效性和临床实用性。

**英文摘要**: Despite their considerable promise, many current medical foundation models remain modality specific and lack transparent reasoning, limiting clinical adoption. To address this gap, we introduce Explainable Vision Language Foundation Model for Medicine (EVLF-FM), a multimodal vision language foundation model that combines broad diagnostic capabilities with fine-grained explainability. The development and testing of EVLF-FM encompassed more than 1.3 million total samples from 18 global datasets across 10 imaging modalities related to six clinical specialties: dermatology, hepatology, ophthalmology, pathology, pulmonology and radiology. External validation employed 13,443 independent test samples from 17 additional datasets across 10 imaging modalities. EVLF-FM supports disease diagnosis, visual question answering and pixel-level visual grounding with reasoning capabilities. In internal validation for disease diagnostics, EVLF-FM achieved the highest average accuracy, 0.895, outperforming leading generalist and specialist models. In medical visual grounding, EVLF-FM also achieved reasonable performance across nine modalities with average accuracy at 0.5 intersection over union (Acc@0.5) of 0.842. External testing further confirmed consistent zero-shot and few-shot performance, with competitive F1 scores despite a smaller model size. A hybrid training strategy combining supervised and visual reinforcement fine-tuning enables EVLF-FM to generate clinically interpretable explanations aligned with visual evidence. Human evaluation of these reasoning outputs using the S.C.O.R.E. framework yielded a high overall mean score of 4.25/5, supporting their clinical interpretability. Current limitations include the need for additional validation across diverse healthcare settings. Prospective clinical studies are required to evaluate the safety, effectiveness and clinical utility of EVLF-FM before routine clinical deployment.
